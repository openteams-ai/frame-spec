# OpenTeams Founder-in-Residence Program

## Purpose

The OpenTeams Founder-in-Residence Program is a lightweight strategic venture-formation path for selected OpenTeams employees, former employees, contractors, and close collaborators who are exploring independent companies that can become aligned partners in the OpenTeams ecosystem.

The program exists because the distributed AI economy cannot be built by OpenTeams alone. OpenTeams is creating the infrastructure, standards, marketplace, and coordination layer for a world in which organizations own their intelligence infrastructure and exchange AI capabilities through governed, reproducible, and accountable systems. That economy will require many independent companies.

The Founder-in-Residence Program helps entrepreneurial people connected to OpenTeams move from employee, contractor, or collaborator roles into founder and ownership roles while preserving clear boundaries around IP, employment, customers, resources, compensation, governance, and commercial alignment.

## Strategic Thesis

OpenTeams should not try to employ every expert, own every implementation company, or internalize every opportunity.

The distributed AI economy needs many aligned companies that can:

- deploy and operate Intelligence Hubs;
- build vertical AI solutions;
- publish reusable Ops;
- develop specialized Cogs;
- author Frames;
- provide managed intelligence services;
- train the applied AI workforce;
- build complementary ecosystem infrastructure.

The Founder-in-Residence Program is how OpenTeams turns entrepreneurial OpenTeams-connected talent into aligned ecosystem founders.

The program is not a side project, retention perk, or informal permission structure. It is a strategic ecosystem-formation mechanism.

## Relationship to Open Technology Incubator

The OpenTeams Founder-in-Residence Program is distinct from Open Technology Incubator.

Open Technology Incubator remains a broader platform for supporting entrepreneurs, companies, and opportunities across a wider opportunity set.

The OpenTeams Founder-in-Residence Program is narrower. It exists for opportunities that are directly coupled to OpenTeams’ strategy, customers, marketplace, infrastructure, services, or ecosystem needs.

A Founder-in-Residence venture may be referred to Open Technology Incubator if it needs broader incubation support, capital formation, company-building infrastructure, or additional partner involvement. But Founder-in-Residence ventures do not automatically belong to Open Technology Incubator and do not require Open Technology Incubator approval unless OTI resources, brand, capital, personnel, or formal support are being used.

## Core Principle

OpenTeams should create more ownership in the ecosystem without losing coherence, trust, or control over its core standards and strategic direction.

This program should help entrepreneurial people become owners while protecting OpenTeams and strengthening the OpenTeams ecosystem.

## What the Program Is

The program is:

- a lightweight strategic venture-formation path;
- a founder transition framework;
- an OpenTeams ecosystem development mechanism;
- a way to create aligned partner companies;
- a structured path for entrepreneurial employees and collaborators;
- a practical tool for increasing ownership in the distributed AI economy.

## What the Program Is Not

The program is not:

- a competing incubator;
- a replacement for Open Technology Incubator;
- a general-purpose startup accelerator;
- an automatic investment vehicle;
- a promise of funding;
- a guarantee of OpenTeams customer access;
- a guarantee of OpenTeams partnership;
- a way to transfer OpenTeams IP without written approval;
- permission for employees to use OpenTeams time, systems, customers, or confidential information for side projects without approval.

## Eligible Participants

Potential participants may include:

- current OpenTeams employees;
- former OpenTeams employees;
- OpenTeams contractors;
- OpenTeams advisors;
- close collaborators;
- technical leaders in the OpenTeams ecosystem;
- operators who are already working near OpenTeams customers, products, or strategic initiatives.

Participation should be selective. The program is for people who have both entrepreneurial potential and a venture idea that could strengthen OpenTeams.

## Eligible Venture Categories

Founder-in-Residence companies should be evaluated based on how they strengthen the OpenTeams distributed AI economy.

### Hub Deployment Companies

Companies that help customers deploy, configure, operate, and maintain Intelligence Hubs.

These companies create implementation capacity and make owned intelligence practical for enterprises, governments, and regulated organizations.

### Vertical Solution Companies

Companies that build industry-specific Ops, Cogs, Frames, integrations, and services for markets such as federal, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, education, energy, legal, and scientific computing.

### Op Publishing Companies

Companies that create installable, versioned, supervised workflows that customers can run inside their own Intelligence Hubs.

These companies turn repeatable business processes into marketplace-distributable AI-enabled applications.

### Cog Development Companies

Companies that specialize in building AI workers for specific tasks, domains, or regulated workflows.

These companies create reusable units of AI labor that can be deployed inside Ops or used directly by customers.

### Frame Authoring and Methodology Companies

Companies that encode expertise, standards, methodologies, brand systems, compliance rules, or operating practices into Frames that can be shared, licensed, or adopted across organizations.

### Managed Intelligence Service Providers

Companies that operate AI workflows, Hubs, Cogs, Ops, integrations, and governance processes on behalf of customers that need outcome delivery rather than only software deployment.

### Training and Workforce Formation Companies

Companies that train the people who will deploy, operate, govern, and extend the distributed AI economy.

These companies may work closely with the Applied AI Society, certification programs, and local implementation communities.

### Ecosystem Infrastructure Companies

Companies that build complementary tooling around Nebari, Nebi, Hub operations, marketplace publishing, observability, auditability, compliance, evaluation, or integration.

## Program Stages

### Stage 1: Exploration

The participant is still primarily an OpenTeams employee, contractor, or collaborator. The goal is to explore whether the idea should become an independent company.

Key questions:

- What is the venture idea?
- Why should this be an independent company rather than an OpenTeams internal project?
- How does it strengthen OpenTeams?
- Who would own and lead the company?
- What customers or markets would it serve?
- What OpenTeams resources, if any, are involved?
- Are there IP, employment, confidentiality, or customer conflicts?

Expected output:

- one-page Founder-in-Residence opportunity memo;
- initial approval to continue exploration;
- clear boundaries on time, resources, confidentiality, and customer contact.

### Stage 2: Formation

The participant begins shaping the company more concretely. This may include naming the company, defining ownership, building a business plan, forming a legal entity, and preparing for transition out of OpenTeams employment if needed.

Key questions:

- What is the company’s initial business model?
- What is the relationship to OpenTeams?
- What assets belong to OpenTeams?
- What assets belong to the new company?
- Is an IP carve-out needed?
- Will the founder remain employed, move part-time, become a contractor, or leave OpenTeams?
- Does OpenTeams receive equity, warrants, revenue share, referral fees, or other economics?
- Does the company need a partner agreement, marketplace agreement, services agreement, or license?

Expected output:

- founder transition plan;
- IP and confidentiality review;
- draft commercial relationship model;
- decision on whether OpenTeams, Travis, OTI, or another entity has any economic participation;
- written approval before any use of OpenTeams resources, customer relationships, or IP.

### Stage 3: Partnership

The company becomes independent and operates as an OpenTeams-aligned partner.

Possible relationship types:

- marketplace publisher;
- implementation partner;
- referral partner;
- reseller partner;
- services subcontractor;
- technology integration partner;
- joint go-to-market partner;
- strategic collaborator.

Expected output:

- signed commercial agreement;
- defined partner category;
- clear customer engagement rules;
- clear branding and messaging rules;
- operating rhythm for collaboration with OpenTeams;
- review cadence for strategic alignment.

## Default Operating Model

Each Founder-in-Residence should have:

- a named OpenTeams sponsor;
- a one-page opportunity memo;
- written participation approval;
- clear employment and time boundaries;
- clear IP and confidentiality boundaries;
- a defined transition plan if the person is currently employed by OpenTeams;
- a target partner model;
- a lightweight monthly review.

The program should not require a large committee, application process, cohort model, curriculum, or demo day.

## Governance Rules

The program is sponsored by OpenTeams and managed directly by Travis or a designated OpenTeams leader.

Approval is required for:

- participation by current employees;
- use of OpenTeams resources;
- any customer contact on behalf of the new venture;
- any use of OpenTeams brand, marks, collateral, or marketplace positioning;
- any IP carve-out or assignment;
- any investment, equity, warrant, revenue-share, or advisory arrangement;
- any commercial agreement between OpenTeams and the new company;
- any employment transition.

Open Technology Incubator does not need to approve Founder-in-Residence participation unless OTI resources, capital, brand, staff, or formal incubation support are involved.

## Legal and Business Boundaries

Participation in the program does not override existing employment agreements, contractor agreements, confidentiality obligations, invention assignment agreements, customer obligations, or fiduciary duties.

The following must be handled carefully and in writing:

- intellectual property ownership;
- use of OpenTeams confidential information;
- use of OpenTeams equipment or systems;
- customer introductions;
- customer ownership and account control;
- employee working time;
- conflicts of interest;
- compensation and equity;
- founder transition from employee to independent operator;
- use of OpenTeams brand or endorsement;
- open-source licensing and contribution policies.

## Default Policy

Unless a separate written agreement says otherwise:

- participation does not provide funding;
- participation does not transfer OpenTeams IP;
- participation does not grant customer access;
- participation does not grant use of OpenTeams brand;
- participation does not create automatic equity rights for OpenTeams or the founder;
- participation does not require Open Technology Incubator involvement;
- every participant starts with a one-page opportunity memo;
- every current employee needs written approval before using work time or OpenTeams resources;
- every venture needs a separate written agreement before becoming an OpenTeams commercial partner;
- legal review is required for IP, employment transition, equity, or customer-facing activity.

## Founder-in-Residence Opportunity Memo

Each opportunity should begin with a short memo covering the following sections.

### Founder

Who is the proposed Founder-in-Residence?

### Venture Concept

What company are they exploring?

### Strategic Fit

How would this company strengthen OpenTeams?

### Independence Rationale

Why should this be an independent company rather than an internal OpenTeams project?

### Target Customers

Who would the company serve?

### OpenTeams Relationship

What kind of partnership might eventually make sense?

### Distributed AI Economy Role

What role would this company play in the distributed AI economy?

Use one or more of the following categories:

- Hub deployment company;
- vertical solution company;
- Op publishing company;
- Cog development company;
- Frame authoring and methodology company;
- managed intelligence service provider;
- training and workforce formation company;
- ecosystem infrastructure company;
- other aligned ecosystem company.

### IP and Resource Boundaries

What OpenTeams assets, systems, customer relationships, confidential information, or work product may be implicated?

### Employment Status

Is the person currently an employee, contractor, former employee, or external collaborator?

### Transition Plan

What needs to happen if the person moves from employee, contractor, or collaborator into founder/operator status?

### Economics

Are there any proposed equity, warrant, revenue share, advisory, referral, reseller, marketplace, or investment economics?

### Open Questions

What legal, financial, customer, operational, or strategic issues need to be resolved?

## Economic Models

The program does not require a single economic model. Each venture should be handled based on its facts.

Possible models include:

### No Equity Model

OpenTeams supports the founder because the company strengthens the ecosystem.

OpenTeams receives no equity but may benefit through partnership, referrals, marketplace revenue, services revenue, or strategic expansion.

### Advisor Equity Model

OpenTeams, Travis, or another agreed party receives advisor equity or warrants in exchange for support, mentorship, introductions, strategic guidance, or early company formation help.

### Strategic Partner Model

OpenTeams receives preferred commercial terms, revenue share, referral fees, reseller margin, marketplace fees, integration rights, or other commercial benefits.

### Incubator Referral Model

If the company needs broader incubation, it may be referred to Open Technology Incubator under a separate arrangement.

### Investment Model

OpenTeams, Open Technology Incubator, Travis, or affiliated entities may invest, but investment is not automatic and should be handled separately.

## Evaluation Criteria

When evaluating a Founder-in-Residence opportunity, ask:

- Does this venture increase ownership in the OpenTeams ecosystem?
- Does it strengthen the distributed AI economy?
- Does it help customers own their intelligence infrastructure?
- Does it create reusable capabilities that can be packaged, deployed, operated, or exchanged?
- Does it create marketplace density?
- Does it increase implementation capacity?
- Does it open a valuable vertical, geography, customer segment, or capability area?
- Is the founder credible, capable, and aligned?
- Is the venture better outside OpenTeams than inside OpenTeams?
- Are IP, customer, employment, and conflict issues manageable?
- Is the OpenTeams relationship clear enough to proceed?

## Success Measures

The program should be judged by whether it creates aligned, independent companies that strengthen OpenTeams.

Possible success measures:

- viable partner companies formed;
- marketplace offerings created;
- implementation capacity created;
- vertical solutions launched;
- revenue influenced or generated through partner companies;
- employees successfully transitioned into founder roles;
- strategic opportunities moved outside OpenTeams rather than requiring internal staffing;
- ecosystem density around OpenTeams increased;
- founders remain aligned with OpenTeams;
- IP, employment, and governance disputes are avoided.

## Guidance for AI Assistants and Cogs

When helping with Founder-in-Residence work, apply this Frame as operating context.

Prefer recommendations that:

- increase ownership while preserving trust;
- create aligned independent companies rather than unnecessary internal OpenTeams projects;
- protect OpenTeams IP, customer relationships, confidential information, and strategic coherence;
- keep the program lightweight;
- distinguish the program from Open Technology Incubator;
- connect venture ideas to the distributed AI economy;
- clarify the participant’s role, venture category, OpenTeams relationship, and boundary conditions;
- turn ambiguity into a short opportunity memo rather than a heavy process.

Avoid recommendations that:

- imply automatic funding, equity, customer access, IP transfer, or OpenTeams endorsement;
- blur the line between OpenTeams and Open Technology Incubator;
- create unnecessary bureaucracy;
- encourage employees to use OpenTeams resources or customer relationships without written approval;
- treat the program as a generic accelerator;
- ignore legal, employment, IP, or conflict-of-interest issues.
